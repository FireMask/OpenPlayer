# OpenPlayer PCB Rev A — Project Requirements

**Document type:** Hardware requirements and design brief  
**Status:** Designed in SPEC 22 (`specs/22-carrier-pcb.md`, sources in `hardware/pcb/`)  
**Target CAD:** KiCad 10  
**Target manufacturer:** JLCPCB  
**Main controller:** Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W  
**Display:** 1.28-inch round TFT, GC9A01, 240 × 240, SPI  
**Battery concept:** Single-cell Li-ion/LiPo, nominal 3.7 V, planned capacity 5000 mAh  
**Charging module:** FM5324GA USB-C charge/boost board (DWEII, Amazon MX B09YD5C9QC), 5 V / 2 A output  
**Revision:** A

---

## 1. Project goal

Design a custom printed circuit board (PCB) for a portable, modular DIY MP3 player named **OpenPlayer**. The current prototype works at a basic level on a Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W. The PCB is a **bridge board**: it contains no active circuitry, only reliable, labeled, serviceable connectors for:

- Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W, mounted beside the board and connected with a 40-pin IDC ribbon.
- Six physical control buttons plus a power-on key, on a separate button board.
- A 1.28-inch round GC9A01 240 × 240 SPI TFT display ("1.28 TFT ver 1.0", 7-pin header, no backlight pin), plugged directly into the PCB.
- The 5 V output and `K` (key) pad of the charging module, connected by short wires.
- A panel-mount power switch, connected by short wires.

The battery connects straight to the charging module and never touches this PCB.

## 2. Existing software and system architecture

The PCB is hardware interconnect and power distribution; it is not intended to replace the Raspberry Pi or run application software itself.

The Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W will continue running Raspberry Pi OS and the existing MP3-player software architecture:

- MPD as the playback engine and source of playback state.
- A central C#/.NET service for player logic.
- A Python GPIO hardware agent for physical button input and display rendering (pins configurable in `player.json`; defaults below).
- SSH for administration.
- SPI display control for the GC9A01.
- Existing USB DAC for audio output.
- Bluetooth headphone output is a desired capability of the software/system, not a PCB audio codec requirement.

Do not move the project to an ESP32 as part of this PCB revision.

## 3. Design principles

1. **Modular:** the Pi, battery, charging module, display, and button board are replaceable without reworking the PCB.
2. **Safe power design:** no direct connection from a Li-ion cell to the Pi's 5 V supply input. Only the module's regulated 5 V reaches the PCB.
3. **Power-path:** handled by the charging module; the PCB adds none.
4. **Stable 5 V rail:** both Pi 5 V pins and all GND pins carry power over the ribbon; optional bulk capacitor C1.
5. **Serviceable:** keyed/polarized connectors, clear silkscreen labels, mounting holes, and test points.
6. **KiCad source-first:** schematic and PCB are generated by scripts in `hardware/pcb/scripts/` and kept under version control.
7. **Fabrication-ready:** Gerbers and drill files for JLCPCB only after ERC/DRC and physical review.
8. **Do not assume module pinouts:** verify each purchased module's exact pin labels, connector pitch, voltage limits, and datasheet before ordering.

## 4. Functional blocks

### 4.1 Raspberry Pi connection — J1

- J1: 2 × 20, 2.54 mm shrouded (keyed) box header, connected to the Pi's header with a straight-through 40-pin IDC ribbon (≤ 10 cm). J1 pin N = Pi physical pin N.
- The Pi is powered through header pins 2 and 4 (5 V) with all 8 GND pins (6, 9, 14, 20, 25, 30, 34, 39).
- **Never plug power into the Pi's micro-USB port while the ribbon is connected** (dual feed). The silkscreen carries this warning.
- Measure 5 V at the Pi under load at bring-up; it must stay ≥ 4.75 V.

### 4.2 Display connector — J5

Display: GC9A01, 1.28-inch round TFT, 240 × 240, SPI. J5 is a 1 × 7, 2.54 mm female header; the module plugs in directly from the front of the board.

7-position interface, in the module's silkscreen order. Matches the validated prototype wiring (`docs/gpio-map.md` §10) and `player.json` defaults (`hardware.display.dc` = 16, `rst` = 26):

| J5 pin | Module label | Signal | Raspberry Pi BCM GPIO | Pi physical pin |
|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | VCC | 3V3 | — | 1, 17 |
| 2 | GND | GND | — | any GND |
| 3 | SCL | SPI0 SCLK | GPIO11 | 23 |
| 4 | SDA | SPI0 MOSI | GPIO10 | 19 |
| 5 | DC | Data/command | GPIO16 | 36 |
| 6 | CS | SPI0 CE0 | GPIO8 | 24 |
| 7 | RST | Reset | GPIO26 | 37 |

Important:
- SDA/SCL on this module are SPI data/clock, not I²C.
- VCC and logic are 3.3 V only; never 5 V.
- The module has no backlight (BL) pin: the backlight is on whenever VCC is present, including after the Pi shuts down. The charging module's auto-off (load < 50 mA) or the J6 power switch cuts it. A panel VCC MOSFET switch is left for a future spec.
- DC/RST were moved from GPIO5/GPIO6 (pins 29/31) to GPIO16/GPIO26 (pins 36/37) after a short between adjacent header pins 29–31 on the prototype. Do not revert without updating `player.json` defaults.
- SPI0 is in use, so GPIO7–11 are not free for other functions.

### 4.3 Button board connector — J4

One 8-pin JST-XH connector to a separate button board with six normally-open momentary buttons, the power-on key, and a shared ground.

| J4 pin | Signal | Raspberry Pi BCM GPIO | Pi physical pin |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | GND common | — | GND |
| 2 | PLAY / PAUSE | GPIO17 | 11 |
| 3 | PREVIOUS | GPIO27 | 13 |
| 4 | NEXT | GPIO22 | 15 |
| 5 | VOL_UP | GPIO23 | 16 |
| 6 | VOL_DOWN | GPIO24 | 18 |
| 7 | MENU | GPIO25 | 22 |
| 8 | PWR_KEY | — (to J3, module `K`) | — |

Pin assignment matches `player.json` defaults and `docs/gpio-map.md`.

Button behavior:
- Each normally-open button connects its signal line to GND while pressed.
- GPIO inputs use the internal pull-up in software; no external resistors.
- `PWR_KEY` is **not** a Pi GPIO. It goes only to the charging module's `K` pad (via J3), so power-on works with the Pi off.

### 4.4 Battery and charging module

- Battery: one Li-ion/LiPo cell, 3.7 V nominal, planned 5000 mAh, with built-in protection. It connects straight to the module's `+` / `−` battery pads.
- Charging module: FM5324GA USB-C board. Per seller listings (unverified against a datasheet):
  - USB-C input 5 V, charge current up to 2.4 A, cut-off 4.2 V.
  - Integrated boost: 5 V regulated output, up to 2 A, ~92.5 % efficiency at 3.6 V in.
  - A short press on `K` turns the output on; the output turns off when the load stays below ~50 mA.
  - 4 battery-level LEDs. A USB-A output on the back is not used.
- Pads used: `5V +` / `−` (output) → J2; `K` → J3. Wires are short; the module sits wherever the enclosure allows.
- The Steren ARD-343 (TP4056 type) was rejected: battery-voltage output only, no power-path, 2 A trip limit.

### 4.5 Power entry and switch — J2, J6

- J2 `PWR_IN` (JST-XH 2-pin): module 5V / GND → `5V_IN`.
- J6 `SWITCH` (JST-XH 2-pin): panel-mount rocker/toggle rated ≥ 3 A, in series between `5V_IN` and `5V_SYS`.
- `5V_SYS` feeds J1 pins 2 and 4, C1 (470–1000 µF / 10 V, optional populate) and C2 (100 nF).
- Power tracks (`5V_IN`, `5V_SYS`, `GND`) are ≥ 1.0 mm.

### 4.6 Expansion and future additions

- Keep GPIO2/GPIO3 available for potential I²C devices.
- OLED is not part of this revision; the current screen target is the GC9A01 TFT.
- Do not add an audio DAC to this PCB; the current prototype uses an existing USB DAC.

## 6. GPIO conflict review

| Function | BCM GPIO | Physical pin |
|---|---:|---:|
| Display MOSI | 10 | 19 |
| Display SCLK | 11 | 23 |
| Display CS / CE0 | 8 | 24 |
| Display DC | 16 | 36 |
| Display RST | 26 | 37 |
| PLAY / PAUSE | 17 | 11 |
| PREVIOUS | 27 | 13 |
| NEXT | 22 | 15 |
| VOL_UP | 23 | 16 |
| VOL_DOWN | 24 | 18 |
| MENU | 25 | 22 |

Electrical constraints:
- Raspberry Pi GPIO is 3.3 V logic and is not 5 V tolerant.
- All modules share the PCB ground.
- Keep GPIO17/22/23/24/25/27 (buttons), GPIO7–11 (SPI0) and GPIO16/26 (display) reserved. The hardware agent rejects duplicate pins in `player.json`.
- `hardware/pcb/scripts/netlist.py` asserts that the PCB nets match `config/player.example.json`.

## 7. PCB layout

- Two layers, GND pour on both sides, JLCPCB default rules.
- Board 60 × 45 mm (`BOARD_W` / `BOARD_H` in `build_pcb.py`); the 2 × 20 header (~56 mm) sets the minimum width. Final size depends on the enclosure.
- J5 on the front (display faces the enclosure front); J1, J2, J3, J4, J6 and C1 on the back, cable connectors on the edges.
- Keep SPI away from the 5 V entry.
- Silkscreen: board name, "Rev A", connector names, pin 1, J4/J5 signal names, and the micro-USB warning.

## 8. KiCad project and fabrication workflow

See `hardware/pcb/README.md`. In short: `netlist.py` (single source of truth) → `build_sch.py` → `build_pcb.py` → `export-fab.ps1`, with ERC and DRC (schematic parity) through `kicad-cli`. Do not treat a clean ERC/DRC report as proof that the power circuit is safe.

## 9. Validation and bring-up checklist

### Before fabrication
- [ ] Display module pinout and header pitch verified against the physical module.
- [ ] Button board pin order matches J4.
- [ ] Battery chemistry, voltage limits, current capability and protection verified.
- [ ] Charging module pads (`5V +`, `−`, `K`, battery) identified on the real board.
- [ ] ERC clean or every remaining warning understood.
- [ ] DRC clean or every remaining warning understood.
- [ ] 3D renders and connector orientation reviewed.
- [ ] Gerbers and drill files inspected in JLCPCB's viewer.

### First power-up
- [ ] Do not connect the Raspberry Pi initially.
- [ ] Inspect PCB for shorts, solder bridges, reversed connectors and incorrect polarity.
- [ ] Verify battery polarity at the module with a meter.
- [ ] Verify 5 V at TP2 with the switch on, and 0 V with it off.
- [ ] Check heating and voltage droop under a test load.
- [ ] Only then connect the Pi and check for stable boot; measure 5 V at the Pi under load (≥ 4.75 V).
- [ ] Plug and unplug USB-C while playing; if the Pi resets, populate C1 and retest.
- [ ] Measure halted current after `power_off`; confirm whether the module auto-shuts off.
- [ ] Test screen, each button, `PWR_KEY`, USB DAC, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth.

## 10. Open questions

1. Exact battery model, connector, maximum discharge current, and protection.
2. FM5324GA datasheet: confirm charge-while-discharge without output dropout.
3. Whether the module auto-shuts off with the Pi halted (> 50 mA would keep it on).
4. Final PCB dimensions relative to the enclosure.
5. Whether to add a panel VCC switch for the display (module has no BL pin).

## 11. Explicit non-goals for Rev A

- Replacing the Raspberry Pi with an ESP32.
- Designing a custom audio DAC; the current USB DAC remains external.
- Adding the OLED display; the GC9A01 TFT is the target for this revision.
- Designing a charger or boost circuit on the PCB.
- Battery voltage sense / low-battery signal to the Pi.
- The button board PCB.
- Fabricating a production batch before electrical, mechanical, and thermal validation.

**Safety note:** This is a requirements brief, not a verified circuit design. The charging module's behavior must be validated against its datasheet and tested before connecting the Raspberry Pi or enclosing the battery.
